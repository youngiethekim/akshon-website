Should Steve and Kazuya be banned from Smash Ultimate tournaments? This question has divided the competitive fighting game community as in-person events return and these powerful DLC characters face real LAN conditions for the first time. When online-only play dominated the pandemic era, Steve's mining mechanics and Kazuya's devastating command grabs felt manageable. Now that tournaments are back on LAN, the debate over whether these characters deserve bans has intensified dramatically.

The shift from online to offline competition revealed something crucial: Steve and Kazuya perform significantly differently under tournament conditions. Without lag compensation, their frame data becomes even more oppressive. Players who thrived online suddenly faced a harsh reality. At GOML, one of the year's premier Smash Ultimate events, we spoke directly with top competitors, judges, and community members to understand both sides of this heated discussion.

## The Case For Banning Steve and Kazuya

Proponents of a ban argue that these characters possess inherent design flaws that undermine competitive integrity. Steve's block placement creates suffocating neutral game scenarios that certain matchups cannot overcome. Kazuya's launcher combos convert minor hits into devastating stocktakes. Many argue that banning problematic characters protects the competitive ecosystem and ensures fair tournament play.

## Why Some Players Oppose a Ban

Opponents counter that banning characters sets a dangerous precedent. They point to dedicated players who've adapted their strategies and found ways to compete despite unfavorable matchups. This camp believes that asking players to improve rather than removing characters maintains the game's original design integrity.

## What Tournament Organizers Think

Tournament organizers face immense pressure from both sides. Banning characters risks alienating players invested in those characters, while allowing them might drive others away. The decision impacts sponsorships, attendance, and the scene's long-term health.

## The Community's Divided Opinion

When we spoke with the Smash community at GOML, opinions were split almost evenly. Some competitors felt certain matchups were genuinely unwinnable, while others believed players simply needed more time to adapt. Casual fans worried about the game's future, while some established pros argued balance issues were overblown.

The Steve and Kazuya ban debate ultimately reflects a deeper question: what kind of competitive Smash Ultimate scene do players want? The answer will determine tournament rulesets for years to come.
