Speedrunning has produced some of the most mind-blowing discoveries in gaming history. These iconic speedrunning moves didn't just shave seconds off world records—they fundamentally transformed how players interact with their favorite games. From glitches that developers never anticipated to techniques that exploit physics engines, these breakthroughs defined entire communities and continue to shape competitive play today.

## The Backwards Long Jump That Started It All

When you mention iconic speedrunning moves, Super Mario 64's backwards long jump (BLJ) is the glitch that changed everything. This technique isn't just famous—it's the discovery that proved speedrunning could be legitimate competitive play. Players found that by repeatedly jumping backwards on slopes, they could accumulate momentum with no speed cap in the negative direction, turning Mario into what speedrunners call "an intercontinental ballistic missile."

The backwards long jump unlocked impossible paths through the game. It let runners skip the Endless Stairs by building enough speed to phase through invisible checkpoints. Within years, players reduced the star requirement from 70 to zero, completely rewriting how the game could be completed. This single technique became the blueprint for discovering glitches in 3D games.

## Bunny Hopping: The Source Engine Foundation

If the backwards long jump defined 3D speedrunning, bunny hopping became the heartbeat of first-person shooter speedruns. Originating in Quake, this technique appears in Half-Life, Portal, Left 4 Dead, and Counter-Strike. The bunny hopping method works by gaining air momentum through strafing and jumping on the first possible frame, tricking the game into maintaining speed that should be impossible.

Speedrunners bind jump to mouse scroll wheels for consistency. In Half-Life 2, developers capped forward velocity at just under 400 units—but forgot to cap backwards movement, allowing speeds ten times higher. Counter-Strike took bunny hopping further, spawning entire mini-games and obstacle courses around the technique.

## Sword Flying and the Halo 2 Revolution

Halo 2's sword flying exploits the energy sword's lunge mechanic. By swapping weapons and reloading within a two-frame window, players trigger the lunge at impossible distances. This technique revolutionized Halo 2 speedrunning until 2005, when a system update patched it from multiplayer. Remarkably, in 2018, 343 Industries reintroduced sword flying in Halo 2 Anniversary Edition, revitalized the speedrunning scene and opened entirely new routes.

## The Wrong Warp That Changed Everything

Ocarina of Time's Gondor wrong warp might be speedrunning's most revolutionary discovery. By dying at the exact frame you defeat a boss and manipulating the game's cutscene timer, runners warp to unintended locations—even straight to Ganon's Castle. When this glitch was discovered in 2012, it invalidated over a decade of optimization. Every runner had to master this frame-perfect glitch to remain competitive, reshaping the speedrunning landscape forever.
