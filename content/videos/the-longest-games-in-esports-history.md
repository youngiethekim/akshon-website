# The Longest Esports Games Ever Played

Esports has produced some incredible moments, but nothing quite captures the drama of a match that simply refuses to end. The longest esports games in history showcase both incredible skill and bizarre situations that break the very fabric of competitive gaming. From professional Counter-Strike marathons spanning nearly three hours to games that lasted multiple days, these matches pushed players, casters, and the games themselves to their absolute limits. Let's dive into the matchups that defied all expectations.

## CS:GO's 88-Round Longest Match

The longest professional Counter-Strike match happened between Team Xenex and XL Esports in the ESL UK Premiership. What should have been a dominant performance turned into an 88-round monstrosity on Inferno. Xenex controlled most of the map, going up 10-5 at halftime, then 14-8 on the T-side. But Excel refused to quit.

The rounds stretched on endlessly—15-13, then into overtime. Neither team could secure the three consecutive rounds needed for victory. The scoreboard kept climbing: 21-21, 24-24, 33-33, 40-40. Every single player exceeded 50 kills. After nearly three hours, Xenex finally closed it out 46-42, setting what many thought was an unbreakable record for a professional CS:GO match.

## League of Legends' 94-Minute Epic

During the 2018 LCK Spring season, JAG and SKT T1 played a game that would become legendary. Game three went to 94 minutes and 37 seconds—the longest League of Legends game ever recorded. The meta at the time favored scaling and wave clear, making decisive team fights nearly impossible to win.

Jin Air's ADC Teddy accumulated an absurd 1,465 CS, a record that may never be broken. Both teams kept farming instead of fighting. Casters had to keep viewers entertained for an hour and a half of stalling. Finally, at 93 minutes, Faker got caught, and Jin Air punished the mistake to end the longest LoL match in competitive history.

## Dota 2's 3-Hour Glitch Fest

In Star Series Season 12, Cloud9 versus Scary Faces created pure chaos. The game lasted 3 hours and 20 minutes, and it literally broke Dota 2. Strange itemization appeared—Scary Faces dropped gems in random locations, and disruptors ran Mask of Madness. Players experimented with bizarre builds just to pass the time.

The casters nearly lost their minds. One hero, Bristleback, literally caught on fire randomly due to engine glitches. Players even began negotiating with each other to end the nightmare. Eventually, Scary Faces pushed into Cloud9's base and finished it—mercifully.

## StarCraft 2 and Age of Empires 2 Marathons

A Zerg versus Protoss matchup in the WCS European Challenger league went on for over 3 hours, with Firebake's Swarm Hosts spawning endless trash units while Mana's Protoss army remained expensive and fragile. Eventually, Firebake depleted the map's vespene gas reserves, starving Mana out and winning through resource depletion.

But nothing compares to the ranked Age of Empires 2 match between Ruben Stock and Andre2i—a game that lasted 97 hours across multiple days. Both players ran out of resources, and the only way to win was to destroy the opponent's castles. Ruben discovered he could micro-dodge a castle's arrows with his Hussar units, chipping away when Andre slept. Eventually, both sides were at a stalemate with just one castle and town center each. The match continued into day four until Ruben went away for the weekend, and Steam lost connection—ending the 97-hour longest esports game ever.
