Super Smash Bros. Melee stages tell a story about competitive fairness. With 29 playable stages available, tournaments use only 6—meaning roughly 80% of Melee stages are banned. Why does the competitive community enforce such strict restrictions? The answer reveals how stage design directly impacts gameplay balance and determines whether tournaments reward skill or luck.

The six tournament-legal stages—Final Destination, Battlefield, Yoshiâs Story, Dreamland 64, Pokémon Stadium, and Fountain of Dreams—were carefully selected because they provide neutral, predictable conditions for all characters. Each stage removes variables that could unfairly advantage one fighter over another, ensuring player skill decides every match.

## What Makes a Stage Tournament-Legal

A competitive Melee stage must meet strict criteria. Platform placement needs to be symmetrical or balanced enough that no character exploits stage geometry for unearned advantage. Blast zones—the boundaries where players are KO'd—must be consistent on all sides. Most critically, hazards must be either absent or predictable enough that they don't reward luck over decision-making.

Stages like Pokémon Stadium and Fountain of Dreams survived bans because their hazards are manageable and don't disproportionately help specific characters. Predictability is the key to competitive legitimacy.

## Why Most Melee Stages Get Banned

The 23 banned stages fail fundamental competitive requirements in different ways. Hyrule Castle features chaotic platform spawning and unbalanced blast zones. Yoshi's Island's original version has slanted terrain that distorts movement physics. Mushroom Kingdom II includes soft platforms that collapse unpredictably.

Other banned stages create different problems. Kongo Jungle is simply too large, making certain matchups nearly unplayable. Peach's Castle has hazards so severe they overshadow player decision-making entirely. Each removal came from the community discovering competitive problems through thousands of tournament matches.

## How the Ruleset Evolved Over Time

Melee's stage restrictions weren't always this strict. Early competitive play experimented with larger stage pools, but organizers quickly noticed patterns. Players found exploits, certain characters dominated on specific stages, and hazards created frustrating outcomes that masked skill differences.

Decades of competitive tuning refined the current six-stage ruleset. Every banned stage was removed because tournaments revealed genuine problems—not arbitrary preference. The community learned that consistency matters more than variety.

## The Competitive Integrity Question

Stage bans protect tournament integrity by keeping matchups consistent across all games. When only balanced stages appear, a player's placement depends entirely on fundamentals and preparation. This consistency separates competitive Melee from casual play and ensures championships reward the best players.
