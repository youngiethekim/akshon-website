Ever wondered why Melee stages are banned from tournament play? Out of 29 playable stages in Super Smash Bros. Melee, only six are legal in serious competition—meaning roughly 80% get cut. The reason comes down to competitive fairness. Banned stages introduce chaotic hazards, uneven blast zones, and geometry quirks that reward luck instead of skill. The six survivors—Final Destination, Battlefield, Yoshi's Story, Dreamland 64, Pokémon Stadium, and Fountain of Dreams—offer neutral, predictable conditions where a player's fundamentals decide every match.

## What Makes a Stage Tournament-Legal

A legal Melee stage has to clear a strict bar. Platform placement must be symmetrical or balanced enough that no character can abuse the geometry for an unearned edge. Blast zones—the boundaries where players get KO'd—need to be consistent on all sides. Most importantly, stage hazards must be absent or predictable enough that they never reward luck over decision-making. Pokémon Stadium and Fountain of Dreams keep their spots precisely because their hazards are manageable and don't lopsidedly favor specific fighters.

## Why Most Melee Stages Get Banned

The 23 banned stages break these rules in different ways. Hyrule Castle spawns chaotic platforms and has uneven blast zones. The original Yoshi's Island uses slanted terrain that warps movement physics. Mushroom Kingdom II adds soft platforms that collapse unpredictably. Kongo Jungle is simply too large, making certain matchups nearly unplayable, and Peach's Castle has hazards so aggressive they drown out player skill entirely. Each ban came from the community discovering real problems across thousands of tournament sets.

## How the Melee Ruleset Evolved

These restrictions weren't set in stone from day one. Early competitive play experimented with much larger stage pools, but organizers kept spotting the same patterns—exploits, stage-dependent character dominance, and hazards that masked genuine skill gaps. Over two decades of tournament data refined the ruleset down to today's six stages. Nothing was banned on a whim; every removal traces back to a concrete competitive flaw revealed by high-level play.

## Why Stage Bans Protect Competitive Integrity

Stage bans exist to keep matchups consistent from game to game and event to event. When only balanced stages appear, placement depends on preparation and execution rather than a lucky stage draw. That consistency is what separates competitive Melee from casual free-for-alls and ensures championships crown the strongest players.
