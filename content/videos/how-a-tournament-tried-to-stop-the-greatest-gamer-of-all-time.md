Flash is widely considered the greatest gamer of all time, a title earned through dominance that transcended Starcraft itself. But what happens when a tournament organizer decides to stop the unstoppable? This is the remarkable story of how Africa TV Starcraft League attempted to prevent Flash from making esports history by handicapping an entire player.

Flash's dominance in Starcraft was unparalleled. The legendary Terran player holds the record for the longest win streaks in professional Starcraft history and remains the only pro player ever to maintain a 70%+ win rate across all matchups. In 2010 alone, he won four major tournaments and earned the "God" nickname from the community—a title given to only five players in competitive Starcraft history.

## How The Greatest Gamer Returned

After a wrist injury forced Flash into retirement in 2015, his return in 2016 was dramatic. Fans expected him to pick up where he left off, and he did. Flash dominated the Africa TV Starcraft League, winning three consecutive seasons and positioning himself to become the first player ever to claim four consecutive ASL titles. His dominance was so complete that it prompted unprecedented action.

## Africa TV's Map Pool Strategy

Instead of letting history unfold naturally, Africa TV made an unheard-of move. For ASL Season 5, tournament organizers carefully curated the map pool with one goal: disadvantage Terran players. The selected maps—Gladiator, Sparkle, Transistor, and Third World—were specifically chosen because they favored Protoss and Zerg matchups against Terran. This was the greatest gamer's biggest challenge yet.

Flash's response was telling. In an interview before the season, he said the maps "reinvigorated" him, proving the challenge was exactly what he needed. Flash welcomed the impossible odds and prepared to overcome them.

## Snow Ends The Unbeatable Streak

Despite three consecutive titles, Flash couldn't overcome the tournament's designed disadvantage. In the quarterfinals, Snow defeated him 3-2, ending Flash's 11-year undefeated streak against Protoss players in best-of-five series. It was a shocking upset that sent ripples through the esports world.

Yet this story didn't end in defeat. Flash eventually claimed his fourth ASL title in Season 8, getting his revenge against Snow in the finals. This rare situation—where tournament organizers felt compelled to handicap a single player—remains unmatched in esports history.
