The biggest League of Legends match-fixing scandal in esports history shook the Vietnamese Championship Series (VCS) in 2024, exposing one of competitive gaming's most widespread integrity breaches. When investigators uncovered that players from every single team were involved in fixing matches, the region faced an unprecedented crisis that threatened its survival. This wasn't a isolated incident—it was a systemic breakdown affecting half the league's roster.

The scandal began in August 2023 when SBTC, a top-performing team, had a match delayed under mysterious circumstances. Leaked text messages from a 2021 game revealed players coordinating match outcomes and specific in-game events like First Blood and objective timings. The investigation that followed became one of the most dramatic moments in competitive League of Legends history, ultimately leading to massive bans and league-wide suspensions.

## How League of Legends Match-Fixing Actually Worked

Players weren't just betting on who would win or lose matches. They were manipulating prop bets—wagers on specific events like which team gets First Blood, how many dragons spawn, or total towers destroyed. By controlling these micro-events within matches, players could cash in on thousands of dollars while keeping games competitive enough to avoid suspicion. One leaked sequence showed players deliberately prolonging a game to hit over-under betting lines on dragon count and tower destruction, a manipulation that shocked commentators in real time.

## The Investigation That Changed Everything

On March 15–16, 2024, Riot Games conducted what felt like a criminal investigation. Players had their phones confiscated and were individually questioned for eight hours. One player described the experience as feeling like "going to jail." By March 18th, the entire league was suspended. Thirty-two of 68 players—representing all eight teams—faced indefinite suspension. The scope was staggering: the scandal wasn't limited to one team or region; it was league-wide.

## Why VCS Players Risked Everything

VCS players earn around $750 USD monthly—comfortable but not life-changing. Esports careers are notoriously short, typically two to three years at the top before burnout or replacement. Match-fixing betting offered the chance to make tens of thousands monthly during a limited competitive window. The calculation was simple: short-term massive income versus a career ban seemed worth the risk to many players facing uncertain futures.

## The Aftermath and Permanent Bans

By June 2024, 28 players received bans ranging from six months to permanent. Rainbow Warriors had their entire roster permanently banned from Riot events. GAM Esports, a veteran organization that competed at Worlds, had implicated players but survived the purge. The league resumed with skeleton crews of unknown players and retired veterans filling spots.
