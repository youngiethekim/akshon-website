# Granblue Fantasy Versus Rising: A Fighting Game That Got A Second Chance

Granblue Fantasy Versus Rising is a rare example of a fighting game that got a genuine second chance—and this time, it's thriving. The original Granblue Fantasy Versus launched during the COVID-19 pandemic with crippling online connectivity issues that killed its momentum before it could gain traction. But Arc System Works and Cygames didn't give up. Instead, they rebuilt the game from the ground up, and the result is a fighting game that's now a main-stage title at Evo 2024.

## Why The Original Granblue Fantasy Versus Failed

When the first Granblue Fantasy Versus released, it had all the right ingredients: stunning visuals, accessible mechanics, and a unique single-button special-move system designed to lower the barrier of entry for newcomers. The problem? The netcode was so unreliable that competitive players and casual fans alike couldn't reliably play online matches. In the fighting game community, if you can't play online, the game dies—and that's nearly what happened here.

## Granblue Fantasy Versus Rising Introduces Key Gameplay Changes

Granblue Fantasy Versus Rising isn't just a patch; it's a complete evolution. The sequel introduced dash normals that fundamentally changed neutral gameplay, ultimate skills that reward meter spending with flashy 50% combos, and a bravery point system that adds reactionary depth to offense. These changes made Rising feel like a proper anime fighter while maintaining the accessibility that defines the franchise.

But the real game-changer was distribution strategy. Rising launched with a robust free-to-play version offering full access to casual and ranked modes, three rotating weekly characters, and Gran the Shoto permanently unlocked. This barrier-free entry point brought in casual players, gacha fans from the original game, and curious fighting game enthusiasts who might never have bought the $50 version outright.

## Free-To-Play Model Drives Player Acquisition

The free-to-play model solved a critical problem: player acquisition. Friends who played the gacha game could jump into the fighting game without financial risk. Tournament players discovered that Granblue's simple inputs and quick matches made it perfect for multi-game fighting game events where players might only have an hour or two to compete across titles. Rollback netcode ensures stable online play, and guest characters like 2B from NieR: Automata drive crossover appeal.

## Why Granblue Fantasy Versus Rising Matters Now

Balance is nearly flawless, with only a handful of outlier characters needing minor adjustments. The game has solidified itself as a staple of competitive fighting games at major tournaments. What makes this success so remarkable is that it proves fighting games can get second chances if developers commit to fixing core problems. Better netcode, smarter monetization, and genuine mechanical improvements turned a game that fell through the cracks into one audiences actually want to play.
