# The Gamer Who Fought History And Won

For two decades, Korea ruled Starcraft with an iron fist. Then one player changed everything. This is the story of how a Finnish Zerg player became the first non-Korean to claim the title of world's best Starcraft 2 player, ending Korea's seemingly untouchable 20-year dominance in esports' most competitive scene.

## The Korean Dominance That Seemed Unbreakable

To understand just how historic this achievement was, you need to grasp how completely Korea controlled Starcraft. The term "foreigner" itself tells the story—anyone not Korean was automatically considered an outsider in a game that belonged to Korea. Dismissing non-Korean victories as one-off flukes was standard. Before 2018, only two foreigners in Starcraft and Starcraft 2 history had won premier tournaments in Korea: Gur in early Brood War and Neeb at Kespa Cup 2016.

Even Neeb's historic Triple Crown achievement—winning in Europe, North America, and Korea—couldn't launch him into the conversation of being the world's best player. The gap between top foreigners and elite Korean pros remained vast and seemingly insurmountable.

## Breaking Through the Korean Wall

Our protagonist, Serral, started making waves in 2017 with victories over renowned Korean players and solid tournament placements. But like countless foreigners before him, he hit a ceiling when facing Korea's elite. In 2018, everything changed. Serral dominated the entire WCS circuit that year, winning multiple premier events and establishing himself as the undisputed best foreigner alive.

His true test came at GSL versus the World, an incredibly stacked international tournament. Serral swept through the competition, defeating Innovation 3-0 and Dark 3-1 in back-to-back dominant performances. In the finals against Stats, he claimed victory in a best-of-seven series that could have gone either way, but Serral's superior decision-making prevailed.

## Cementing Starcraft Immortality

One tournament victory wasn't enough to silence the skeptics. Serral proved the doubters wrong at BlizzCon's WCS Global Finals later that year, where he swept through all competition—including another rematch with Stats in the grand finals. Serral lifted the trophy as the first-ever non-Korean world champion of Starcraft 2.

This victory sparked a domino effect. By 2019, other talented foreigners like Reynor began rising through the ranks. Today, the tables have turned completely. Where once Koreans ruled, foreigners now occupy the top spots across all three races. The shift from Korean dominance to international competition happened because one player proved it was possible.
