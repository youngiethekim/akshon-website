# Hytale Saved: How One Man Revived a Dead Game

In June 2025, Riot Games announced the cancellation of Hytale—a game that had been in development for over a decade. Hypixel Inc. faced layoffs, the studio appeared doomed, and millions of fans thought their dream was dead. But thanks to one man's determination and a massive personal investment, Hytale was saved from oblivion. This is the story of how a Minecraft mod evolved into a gaming phenomenon, survived corporate mismanagement, and ultimately rose like a phoenix to become one of gaming's greatest comebacks.

## From Minecraft Mod to Gaming Powerhouse

Hytale's origins trace back to the early 2010s when Minecraft YouTubers Simon Colas Laflamme and Philippe Touchette created the Hypixel server. What started as custom maps and mini-games snowballed into one of Minecraft's largest communities. Their polished mini-games like Bed Wars, Sky Wars, and Mega Walls became so popular they spawned an entire YouTube category. By 2013, Hypixel Inc. employed 40 people and generated significant revenue through cosmetic microtransactions. The platform succeeded because it offered more than gameplay—it provided a safe social space where players could hang out with friends.

## The Mojang Problem

Everything changed in 2014 when Mojang updated their EULA, restricting monetization to cosmetics only. Power-ups, gear, and stat-boosting items were banned from paid purchases. This single decision slashed Hypixel's revenue by 85%, forcing Colin Laflamme to realize that true independence required building their own game. In 2015, he began developing Hytale in secret, envisioning a block-based game with what he called the "Disneyland effect"—a complete experience where explorers could discover procedurally generated worlds, gamers could play community servers and mini-games, and modders could create entirely new content.

## The Riot Games Acquisition and Development Hell

When Hytale's first trailer dropped in December 2018, it garnered 31 million views within weeks. Riot Games' acquisition seemed like a dream scenario, but it became a nightmare. Riot's perfectionism and ever-rising standards kept Hytale stuck in development hell for over five years. Features were constantly rebuilt, the engine was rebooted multiple times, and deadlines repeatedly slipped. Fans grew exhausted by endless delays and hollow promises.

## How Colin Laflamme Saved Hytale

When Riot canceled Hytale in 2025, Colin Laflamme immediately announced he would personally invest $25 million to save the game. Within months, he reacquired the IP and rehired 30 developers. He was willing to launch with rough edges, open-source beta testing, and cut some unfinished features—whatever it took to get the game into players' hands. Hytale launched in early access on January 13, 2026, and immediately became Twitch's most-watched game, proving that fans valued playability over corporate perfection.
