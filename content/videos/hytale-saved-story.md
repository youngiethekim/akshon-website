In 2025, Riot Games canceled one of the most anticipated releases of the year. After a decade in development, Hytale was shut down overnight — layoffs, closed doors, dead dream. But the story of how Hytale was saved is one of the most inspiring redemption arcs in gaming, thanks to one founder who refused to let a good thing die.

## From Minecraft Mod to Gaming Phenomenon

The Hytale story starts with two Minecraft YouTubers, Simon "Colin" Laflamme and Philippe Touchette, who built one of the game's biggest communities. Their server, Hypixel, birthed legendary mini-games like Bed Wars, Sky Wars, and Mega Walls. Hypixel wasn't just polished — it nailed what Colin called the "Disneyland effect": people came to hang out with friends as much as to play.

When Mojang changed Minecraft's EULA in 2014, banning gameplay-affecting monetization, Hypixel's revenue cratered by 85%. That was the spark. If they wanted true creative and economic freedom, they'd have to build their own engine, their own game — Hytale.

## How Riot Games Nearly Killed Hytale

The first Hytale trailer dropped in 2018 and racked up 31 million views. Riot Games acquired Hypixel, and hype went supersonic. But Riot's perfectionism sent the project into development hell. Reboots, engine rebuilds, delay after delay — until June 23, 2025, when Riot canceled Hytale entirely and laid off the team.

## How Hytale Was Saved

That should have been the end. Instead, Colin Laflamme pledged $25 million of his own money to save Hytale. He reacquired the IP from Riot, rescued 30 developer jobs, and announced an early access launch for January 13, 2026 — "rough edges and all."

The payoff was massive. Hytale became the most-watched game on Twitch at launch, blending sandbox building, RPG exploration, and a booming CurseForge modding scene. Pre-orders alone funded two more years of development, and fans finally played the game they'd waited over a decade for.
