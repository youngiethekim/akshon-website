# MultiVersus Success: Why It Beat Nickelodeon All-Star Brawl

For over two decades, Super Smash Bros has dominated the platform fighter genre. MultiVersus, Warner Brothers' entry into the competitive space, managed to achieve massive success where Nickelodeon All-Star Brawl stumbled just months after launch. But what exactly separated MultiVersus success from NASB's failure? The answer lies in deliberate game design choices, authentic IP execution, and a willingness to reimagine the platform fighter formula entirely.

## MultiVersus Success Built on 2v2 Team Battles

MultiVersus is fundamentally built around 2v2 team battles rather than traditional 1v1 combat. This architectural choice defines the entire experience and explains much of MultiVersus success in the competitive landscape. The game incentivizes teamwork through distinct character classes and a comprehensive perk system. Support characters like Raindog aren't viable solo fighters but become essential assets in team settings, controlling space and buffing allies. Tank characters like Superman absorb punishment, while assassins like Arya deliver burst damage. This diversity creates meaningful team compositions that simply don't exist in single-player-focused games like Smash Bros.

## Voice Acting Made the Critical Difference

Nickelodeon All-Star Brawl launched with a fatal flaw: no voice lines. Combat felt hollow and lifeless during matches. MultiVersus brought in original voice actors including Matthew Lillard (live-action Shaggy), Maisie Williams, and Joe DiMaggio, lending authentic personality that NASB severely lacked. Rather than building characters around strict canon, Player First Games embraced internet culture—Shaggy references memes about power levels with voice lines like "You're gonna have to use two percent of my power this time." This self-aware approach resonated with modern gamers far more than reverent adaptations.

## Free-to-Play Accessibility Changed Everything

NASB cost $50 at launch—a steep barrier for casual players. MultiVersus going free-to-play proved transformational. Accessibility matters enormously. Players could download and try it risk-free, and the cooperative 2v2 loop appealed to friends who traditionally avoided fighting games. The battle pass system, cosmetics, quality-of-life features like stickers and emotes, and reasonable monetization kept engagement high without feeling predatory. Within weeks, MultiVersus broke concurrent player records for fighting games.

## Creative Character Design That Breaks Convention

MultiVersus doesn't copy Smash's formula. Tom and Jerry function as a true duo character unlike anything in the genre. Bugs Bunny teleports via rabbit holes. Iron Giant breaks traditional size limitations while remaining balanced. Steven Universe spawns protective shields and platforms. This creativity signals that Player First Games understands its own identity—a game that's fun and competitive without taking itself too seriously.

## Corporate Support and Tournament Infrastructure

Warner Bros backed MultiVersus with meaningful investment. A $100,000 prize pool for EVO doubles proved corporate commitment. Ongoing tournament circuits through Twitch Rivals and community events maintain competitive momentum. The developer communicates openly about balance issues and patches aggressively to address problems.
