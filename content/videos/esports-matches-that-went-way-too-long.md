# Longest Esports Matches of All Time

Some esports matches become legendary not for brilliant plays, but for simply refusing to end. The longest esports matches test player endurance, frustrate casters, and sometimes break the games themselves. From technical pauses to server resets, here are the matches that went way too long.

## Valorant's Record-Breaking Split Marathon

In 2021, Gen.G and TSM played one of the most grueling Valorant matches ever broadcast. The map started normally on Split, but by round 40, something unprecedented happened: the game's technical systems couldn't handle it anymore. Valorant lobbies were designed to reset after 90 minutes, and the match was approaching that limit.

The scoreboard showed TSM leading 20-20 when a technical pause was called. The teams had to abandon the lobby and resume in a fresh one—essentially turning a single map into two separate games. After the restart, TSM closed it out 23-21, but the entire sequence consumed over 100 minutes and 44 total rounds. It remains the longest professional Valorant match on record, exposing gaps in the game's competitive infrastructure that developers hadn't anticipated.

## Apex Legends' Marathon Finals

Apex Legends uses a match-point format where the longest esports matches can span multiple games. Teams accumulate points through placements and eliminations, then must win a final round once they hit the threshold. In the APAC North Regional Finals during 2024, this system produced a grueling 13-game series that lasted nearly eight hours.

With 16 of 20 teams eligible to win by game 13, the pressure mounted with each passing round. Teams came close repeatedly—round 10 saw three match-point teams in the final four—but none could seal the deal until Striders finally claimed victory in round 13. It's a testament to how competitive balance, while thrilling, can accidentally create marathon events.

## The 6-Hour Pokemon Battle That Changed the Rules

Perhaps the most infamous longest esports match happened in Smogon's competitive Pokemon scene. On October 6, 2016, ABR faced off against Telly in a ladder tournament match that would last 6 hours and 50 minutes—spanning 1,287 turns.

The match started competitively, but after turn 29, only one Pokemon had fainted. Both players' teams were too bulky to break through, creating a stalemate. For over 1,200 turns, they switched Pokemon, conserved move points (PP), and waited. When ABR finally revealed Skarmory with Swords Dance, the strategy became clear: slowly outlast Telly's PP reserves. By turn 1,261, ABR's Skarmory defeated Telly's last viable Pokemon.

This match directly caused Smogon to implement a hard 1,000-turn limit to prevent future marathons. Telly was eventually banned from the community for toxicity; ABR went on to become one of Smogon's most decorated champions.

## Why Longest Esports Matches Happen

Longest esports matches typically occur in games with defensive mechanics, high player skill parity, or imperfect time management systems. Valorant's Split map favors defenders; Apex's format allows skilled teams to stall indefinitely; Pokemon's stalemate rules didn't prevent deliberate stallers.

Game developers rarely anticipate matches lasting this long, as seen when Valorant's 90-minute lobby limit was hit. The human element—patience, psychology, and sometimes toxicity—can transform a competitive game into an endurance test.
