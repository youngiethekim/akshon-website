Most people think esports means League of Legends, Counter-Strike, or Valorant. But competitive gaming exists in places you'd never expect. There are thriving esports scenes in games you probably didn't know were actually esports—from VR rhythm games to decades-old RPGs. Here's what makes these unexpected esports games so compelling, and why their communities are growing.

## Gorilla Tag: A VR Esport Like Nothing Else

Gorilla Tag sounds ridiculous until you play it. This VR game lets you move as a primate, swinging through forests using your arms. It released in early access in 2021 as a casual experience, but the competitive scene exploded almost immediately. The game's advanced mechanics—wall jumps, momentum-based movement, and terrain exploitation—created a skill ceiling high enough for professional competition.

The main competitive hub is the Competitive Gorilla Tag Discord, where teams compete in seasonal tournaments. Matches use a tag-based format: one team tags while the other runs. Runners must use advanced jukes and vertical terrain to escape, while taggers coordinate to catch everyone before time runs out. The esport even hosted a $1,000 tournament, with team Silly taking home the prize. What started as a meme-worthy VR experience became a legitimate esport with genuine strategic depth.

## Dance Rush Stardom: The Rhythm Game With Judges

If Dance Dance Revolution and Guitar Hero had a baby, it would be Dance Rush Stardom. This arcade rhythm game uses a full-body dance pad and motion tracking to judge not just timing, but actual dancing ability. Unlike traditional rhythm games, you need style—choreography matters as much as hitting notes.

The game found its competitive home at KAC (Konami Arcade Championship), where dancers from around the world compete. Judges score routines on a 50-point scale, combined with your performance score to determine the winner. This makes Dance Rush Stardom unique among esports: you're being graded on artistry, not just mechanical precision. The community is known as one of gaming's most welcoming, with players traveling internationally to practice, compete, and celebrate.

## Diablo II: The 20-Year Esports Veteran

Diablo II might be the most underrated esport in gaming history. Released in 2000, it's had an active competitive ladder for over two decades. The real competition started with patch 1.10 in 2003, which introduced ladder seasons—basically ranked resets that forced players to start fresh characters.

The goal is simple: reach level 99 first during each season. Every few years, Blizzard adds twists like new boss encounters or separate ladder categories. When Diablo II: Resurrected launched in 2021 with updated graphics, it reignited interest in the competitive scene. As of 2024, we're on the 34th official ladder season, making Diablo II one of gaming's longest-running competitive experiences. Players dedicate hundreds of hours grinding through campaigns, optimizing builds, and racing rivals to claim seasonal glory.
