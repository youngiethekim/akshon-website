Fighting games have always pushed the boundaries of balance and design, but some moves transcend broken and enter legendary status. The **most broken fighting game moves** have shaped entire competitive scenes, forced patches, and become the stuff of community legend. Understanding what makes these tools so overpowered teaches us valuable lessons about game design and competitive integrity.

What makes a move truly broken? It's not just raw damage output—though that certainly helps. A genuinely broken move combines overwhelming utility, minimal risk, or damage output so astronomical that no amount of skill can overcome it. These are the moves that made developers say "yeah, we need to fix that."

## Insane Damage That Breaks Balance

Some broken fighting game moves deal damage so outrageous they can end matches in a handful of hits. When a single combo starter leads to half or full-screen damage with minimal execution, the entire game becomes unbalanced around that single tool. These high-damage moves often receive nerfs months or even years after release once the competitive community fully realizes their potential. The sheer damage output makes other strategies irrelevant.

## Low-Risk, High-Reward Cheese Moves

The real villains aren't always the highest-damage moves—they're the ones that offer absurd utility with almost no downside. A move that's safe on block, hits multiple times, creates massive frame advantage, and deals solid damage becomes virtually unpunishable. When opponents literally cannot punish you for throwing out a move, that's when balance truly breaks. These cheese tactics dominate neutral game play.

## Moves That Dominated Entire Metas

Some broken moves became so prevalent that entire competitive scenes revolved around either using them or countering them. These tools forced character selection, dictated tournament play, and made tournaments predictable. The best fighting game developers recognize these patterns and act before the meta becomes one-dimensional.

## Frame Data Nightmares

Technically broken moves often hide in unfair frame data. A move that's plus on block creates a gap that shouldn't exist. A move with startup faster than any punish becomes a constant threat. When the numbers simply don't add up in the defender's favor, that's broken by definition. Frame advantage legends are born this way.
