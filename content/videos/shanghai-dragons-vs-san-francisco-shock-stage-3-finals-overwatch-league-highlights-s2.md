## Shanghai Dragons vs San Francisco Shock: Map Control

Shanghai's edge started with superior map control on Oasis. Their tank and support coordination created impossible positioning problems for the Shock. By locking down high ground and forcing fights on favorable terms, the Dragons dictated the tempo of every engagement. Smurf was stuck playing reactive rather than proactive, never getting the defensive setup he needed to shield his backline from Shanghai's pressure.

## Ultimate Economy Won the Teamfights

What truly separated these two Overwatch League teams was ultimate management. Shanghai built steady advantages through efficient poke damage and systematic resource conservation. The Shock's supports struggled to cycle defensive cooldowns at the right moments, leaving the team exposed during critical pushes. Young-jin's Earthshatter combos paired perfectly with coordinated follow-up damage, snowballing fights the Shock had no clean answer for.

Shanghai's DPS duo maintained astronomical accuracy across the series. Meanwhile, San Francisco's backline — Sinatra especially — faced relentless pressure that forced repeated positioning mistakes. The Dragons' supports kept key players alive at decisive moments, while the Shock's healing simply couldn't out-pace the incoming burst from Shanghai's synchronized attacks.

Once Shanghai established momentum early, the Shock never fully recovered. San Francisco showed flashes of coordination on later maps but couldn't sustain it against a team firing on all cylinders. The Dragons' roster depth proved why they ranked among the elite OWL teams of the era — every player nailed their role, from tank initiations to support positioning to clean DPS finishes.
